# Candidate niches

**These are not the workflow, and they are not opportunities.** They are hypotheses — starting
points for Phase 0 when the user has no category in mind. Every one still has to earn 150 ledger
rows, three-source triangulation, and a score in `scoring.md` before it is anything at all.

Read `principles.md` § "The gaps close fast" before using this list. Anything on a publicly
circulated trend list is, by definition, being read by everyone else on the same day. The
advantage is never the niche — it is the specific, hard-to-find complaint inside it and the buyer
list you can assemble.

Sourced from r/SaaS and r/SEO_Marketing_Offers threads (2026). Quotes are commenters', not
findings.

---

## Tier 1 — strongest fit for this playbook

These have visible labour signals, budgeted buyers, and domain depth that resists cloning.

**Repetitive human work already being outsourced.** Exports, reconciliation, data stitching between
two systems that don't talk.
> "repetitive tasks being outsourced = best saas ideas in 2026 because someone is literally paying
> humans to do something software could handle"

The purest expression of this playbook — the pain is priced before you arrive. Start at
`mining-playbook.md` § Labor signals. Highest confidence entry on this list.

**Vertical AI agents for a specific industry** — legal, healthcare, logistics, insurance.
> "the real traction is in vertical AI agents for specific industries... skipping generic 'AI
> wrapper' ideas"

Defensibility lives entirely in the vertical, not the model. The moat is the domain rules, the
integrations, and the compliance posture. A vertical agent that is really a prompt fails scoring
factor 7.

**Data cleanup / ops tooling for AI readiness.** Getting an existing company's data into a state
where AI can safely be applied to it.
> "data cleanup/ops tooling that makes existing companies actually ready for AI"

Boring, services-adjacent, and budgeted right now by companies with stalled AI initiatives.
Watch the services-vs-software line: if every customer needs bespoke work, you have an agency.

**Niche low-traffic, high-value tools.** Small user groups, workflows that justify $10-50/month.
> "Target small groups of people... you'll be making 10-20k a month if it's genuinely useful"

Compatible with the lifestyle-business path in `why-boring-b2b.md` § The scale caveat. Cap is real
and should be chosen deliberately.

## Tier 2 — real demand, harder positioning

Budgets exist, but these are security/compliance categories where incumbents are large, procurement
is slow, and buyers require references you do not have yet.

**AI agent security and permission control.** What agents can access and do; catching risky actions
and permission drift.
> "Security for AI agents - what they can access/do and catching risky actions"

**AI data leakage detection.** Showing companies what employees are pasting into public AI services.
> "showing companies what sensitive stuff employees are accidentally sharing with ChatGPT/Gemini"

Note this is a crowded lane — CASB and DLP vendors are shipping into it. Scoring factor 7 is the
one to be honest about.

**Deepfake and social-engineering detection.** Verifying suspicious voice/video requests before
money or data moves.
> "verifying suspicious voice/video requests before someone sends money or data"

Real and growing pain with a clear buyer (finance ops, not IT). Detection accuracy is an ongoing
arms race — a permanent R&D cost, not a one-time build.

**Agentic workflow hardening.** Claim-safety, hallucination prevention, multi-agent reliability.
> "Agentic workflow hardening processes or tools, claim-safety, slop or hallucination prevention"

Buyer is other builders, which trips the anti-pattern in `why-boring-b2b.md` § Avoid. Viable only
if the buyer is an enterprise platform team with a compliance mandate and a budget.

## Tier 3 — treat with suspicion

**SEO / AEO / distribution automation for SaaS.**
> "literally the only moat left is distribution... The whole AI search engine optimization or AEO
> business is basically where it's at right now"

The insight is sound and it is why Phase 6 exists. As a *product* category it is saturated, the
buyer is other founders (anti-pattern), and it is being recommended in the same threads that
recommend everything else. Very high bar on factor 7.

**Short-form content generators (Reels/Shorts).**
> "How about reel or shorts generator. I see a lot of adds of new softwares"

"I see a lot of ads for this" is evidence of a crowded market, not an open one. Consumer-adjacent,
thin wrapper, weeks to clone, churns fast. Fails multiple scoring factors before you start. On the
list for completeness only.

## How to use this list

1. Pick 3-5 entries, weighted toward Tier 1 and toward anything where the user has firsthand
   experience or buyer access (`principles.md` § Firsthand experience compounds).
2. Convert each into a **specific vertical and job title** before mining. "Vertical AI agents" is
   not a category; "claims adjusters at 50-200 person P&C carriers" is.
3. Run Phase 1 normally. The list gets you to the starting line and confers no other advantage.
4. If a Tier 1 entry produces fewer than 40 ledger rows in 90 minutes, drop it like any other
   category. Being on a trend list does not exempt it from the kill criteria.
