# Reaching people

How to actually get a stranger in a specific job to spend 20 minutes with you. Claude drafts;
the user sends. Claude never contacts anyone.

The conversation itself — what to ask once you're on the call — is `interview-guide.md`. This file
is only about getting there.

---

## Channels, by yield

Reply rates below are directional orders of magnitude, not measured figures. They move enormously
with specificity and with whether you're asking for expertise or for money. Treat the *ordering*
as the reliable part.

| # | Channel | Rough reply rate | Volume available | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your own network + 2nd-degree intros | 30-50% | 5-20 | First 5 conversations, fast |
| 2 | **Paid interview recruiting** | 80%+ (you're buying it) | 10-30/week | Hard-to-reach titles, on a deadline |
| 3 | **Freelancers who do the manual work** | 40-60% | 10-50 | Understanding the process in depth, cheaply |
| 4 | Hiring managers behind "roles that shouldn't exist" | 10-20% | 20-100 | Budgeted buyers — highest quality |
| 5 | Review authors with public profiles | 10-15% | 20-100 | People who already wrote 200 words on the pain |
| 6 | Community participation (public, not DM) | compounds | unlimited | Repeatable, becomes your distribution channel |
| 7 | LinkedIn by exact job title | 5-15% | hundreds | Volume once the message is dialled |
| 8 | Cold email to a built list | 1-5% | thousands | Volume, needs a real list |
| 9 | Cold Reddit / X DMs | 0-2%, bannable | — | Last resort, only for a specific post |

To reach the Phase 4 bar of 3 rung-4 commitments you need ~60-100 approaches. Do **not** get there
with 100 cold DMs. Get there with 10 warm, 10 paid, 20 job posters, 20 review authors, 40 LinkedIn.

---

### 1. Your network and 2nd degree

Boring and everyone skips it. Search LinkedIn for the job title, filter to 2nd degree, and ask the
mutual for an intro. An intro converts at roughly ten times a cold approach, and the first five
conversations are the ones that teach you how to talk about the problem at all.

Ask the mutual for a forwardable blurb, not a favour: two sentences they can paste.

### 2. Paid interview recruiting — the one most founders don't know exists

You can *buy* conversations with "operations managers at 50-500 person P&C insurance carriers"
this week. Platforms: **User Interviews**, **Respondent**, **Wynter**, **Askable**. You post
screener questions, they source and schedule, you pay a platform fee plus an incentive to the
participant.

Roughly: $30-60 platform fee plus $50-150 incentive per hour for ordinary B2B roles, several
hundred for senior or hard-to-reach titles. Check current pricing — this moves.

Ten interviews for $1-2k, scheduled within a week, is cheap against two months of building the
wrong thing. Use it when you have no access to a vertical and no time to earn it.

Caveat: paid participants are professionally agreeable and some are serial respondents. They are
excellent for understanding a workflow and terrible as evidence of demand. **Never count a paid
interview above rung 3 on the ladder.** They tell you how the job works; they don't tell you
anyone will buy.

### 3. Hire the freelancer who does the work by hand

Badly underused. On Upwork or Fiverr, find someone who does the exact manual task you want to
automate — the reconciliation, the data stitching, the export-and-reformat. Hire them for one hour
at their rate and ask them to walk you through the process on a screen share.

Why it works: they're being paid, so they show up and they're candid. They've done the task for
many clients, so they know which parts break and which clients complain most. And they'll tell you
what they charge, which prices the pain.

$50-100 for the single best hour of process education you can buy. Be straight about why you're
hiring them.

### 4. Hiring managers behind roles that shouldn't exist

The highest-quality contact in this playbook. They wrote a job description that articulates your
problem, they got budget approved for it, and they're actively thinking about it right now. They
also expect inbound about that post.

Find them from the job posting: the poster's name, or search the company plus "hiring manager" plus
the department. See `mining-playbook.md` § Labor signals for finding the posts.

### 5. Review authors

They wrote 200 unprompted words about the pain, which is more than most people will give you on a
call. G2 and Capterra profiles often carry a name, company, and title — enough to find them on
LinkedIn. TrustRadius reviewers are the most detailed.

Quote their exact words back. That single move is most of the reply rate.

### 6. Community participation — slow, compounding, becomes your channel

Not DMs. You show up in the industry sub, forum, or Slack and answer questions in your area for
two or three weeks before you ask anyone for anything. Then when you post "I've read 200 reviews
of X and here's the pattern", people reply, and some of them DM *you*.

This is slower than everything above and it is the only one that turns into distribution later
(`principles.md` § Distribution is the moat). Start it in week one and let it run underneath the
faster channels.

Rules: no product name, no link, no pitch in a public first contact. Give the finding away.

### 7-8. LinkedIn and cold email

Volume channels, worth it once the message is proven on warmer channels. Build the list from an
association directory, a conference attendee page, a "top N companies in X" listicle, or LinkedIn
Sales Navigator. Verify emails before sending — bounce rates wreck a new domain.

Send from a real profile with a real photo and a real company. Three sentences. One follow-up,
4-7 days later, then stop.

### 9. Cold Reddit and X DMs

Last. A founder in the source thread got zero replies from 100 Reddit DMs, and volume DMing gets
accounts banned. Only defensible when someone posted the specific complaint and you reference that
post directly — and even then, reply publicly first and let them come to you.

## What determines reply rate, more than channel

**Reference something specific they wrote or did.** "Saw your review of Tool X where you mentioned
having to re-key invoices every month" beats any subject line optimisation.

**Ask for expertise, not for their opinion on your idea.** People decline "can I get feedback on
my startup" and accept "you know this workflow better than anyone I can find, can I ask you about
it". You are also getting better data, because the second question doesn't invite them to be nice
to you.

**Be small and specific.** "15 minutes" and "three questions" convert; "a quick chat" doesn't.

**Say what's in it for them.** The honest one: you're talking to 20 people in their role and
you'll share what you find. Then actually share it. This is the whole basis of the community
channel later.

**No product, no link, no pitch in message one.** The moment it reads as sales, the reply rate
collapses and the answers get polite.

**Follow up exactly once.** Four to seven days, two sentences, no guilt.

## Scripts

**Review author / forum poster**
> Hi <name> — saw your review of <tool>, where you mentioned <quoted specific thing>. I'm
> researching that exact problem across <industry> and keep hearing it. Not selling anything —
> would you be up for 15 minutes so I can understand how you handle it today? Happy to share what
> I've found from the other conversations.

**Hiring manager on a glue-work job post**
> Hi <name> — I noticed you're hiring a <title>, and the description mentions <the manual work
> between system A and system B>. I'm researching how <industry> teams handle that gap. Would you
> be open to 15 minutes? I've talked to <n> other <industry> ops leads and can share what they're
> doing.

**Freelancer you're hiring for an hour**
> Hi <name> — I'd like to book an hour at your rate, but not for the usual work. I'm researching
> how <task> gets done and you clearly do a lot of it. I'd like you to walk me through your
> process on a screen share and tell me where it breaks. Happy to pay your normal hourly.

**LinkedIn connection note (300 chars)**
> Researching how <role>s handle <specific workflow> — talking to ~20 people in the role, not
> selling anything. Would value 15 min if you're open to it.

**Community reply, public, before any DM**
> We looked at this too. Across ~40 reviews of <tool> and <tool>, <specific thing> came up N
> times — most people end up <workaround>. <One genuinely useful finding they can use today.>
> Curious whether that matches your experience.

**Follow-up, once**
> Hi <name> — bumping this once in case it got buried. Still happy to share what I've learned from
> the other <n> conversations either way.

## The commitment ladder

The point of the conversations. Rank every outcome; only rungs 4-5 count as validation.

| Rung | What happened | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Yeah I'd pay for that" | Politeness. Worth ~nothing. |
| 2 | Booked and attended a 20-min call | Mild interest, free to give. |
| 3 | Sent their actual spreadsheet, screenshots, or process doc | Real pain — they spent effort. |
| 4 | Named a budget line and an approver, or signed an LOI | Strong. This is a buyer. |
| 5 | Pre-paid, or put a card on file for a paid pilot | Proof. |

**Bar to proceed: 3 people at rung 4+, from 60-100 approaches.** Paid-recruiting participants cap
at rung 3 regardless of enthusiasm.

Never ask "would you pay $X?" — it invites a free yes. The questions that move people up the
ladder are in `interview-guide.md`.

**The rung-4 ask, at the end of a good call**
> This is close enough to what I'm building that I'd rather build it with you than guess. If I have
> <the one feature> working in three weeks, would you run it on real data for a month at $<price>?
> I'd want a card on file so I know it's real on both sides.

## Landing-page test, in parallel

Cheap, runs while you interview. One page: the pain in the buyer's own words as the headline, three
bullets, one price, one button. The button goes to a Stripe payment link or a "$1 to hold a pilot
seat" form — **not** an email capture. Email signups are rung 1 wearing a costume.

Traffic comes from the same approach list plus one community. Judge on paid-intent clicks only.
